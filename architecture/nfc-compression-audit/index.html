    NFC Compression Audit¶

    Date: 2026-11-17 · Code audited: frontend develop at 25ec698; backend develop at d6fd83c for the sync behaviour · Benchmark: tool/nfc_compression_audit/ (full tables in its results.md)

    Question and short answer¶

    Does the app use the best available algorithms and methods to compress what it writes to NFC chips?

    The algorithm, yes; the method around it, no.

    • Algorithm. Without a dictionary, DEFLATE at level 9 is within 7 % of the best general-purpose compressor measured (zstd, brotli, LZMA2). Changing the compressor alone gains nothing.
    • Method. What is missing is what works for payloads of 100 to 2,000 bytes: a dictionary shared by writer and reader, an encoding without redundant fields or values stored as text, a short record type, and a fitting rule that does not leave out what fits.

    With techniques the app already has, and no new dependency, today's NDEF messages are 1.6 to 3.2 times larger than they need to be:

    Whole NDEF message, median Today Best practical, optimistic Best practical, pessimistic
    Triage (patient device) 178 B 77 B 90 B
    Guardian copy, no history 496 B 202 B 268 B
    Guardian copy, 1 consultation + 1 vaccine 1,112 B 352 B 645 B
    Guardian copy, 3 + 3 1,773 B 573 B 1,076 B

    Best practical combines typed values without redundant keys, DEFLATE with a preset dictionary (8 KB for the triage, 32 KB for the guardian copy) and an external record type, with the same 28 B of AES-GCM. Pessimistic assumes the dictionary does nothing for free clinical text and names (see Limits).

    Where it matters is the guardian copy. Today, on an NTAG215 (492 B), not even the copy without history fits in 54 % of the records, and on an NTAG216 (872 B) a consultation fits in practically none.

    Payload format 2 as implemented (ADR 005, recommendation 2) keeps the key names, skips integer keys and default omission, which the dictionary makes cheap, and uses a 17-character external type instead of the 8 characters measured here. Measured with its own code, its medians are 89 B for the triage and 375 B for the guardian copy with 1 + 1, against 77 B and 352 B above.

    Scope¶

    Read:

    • the write and read paths of both chips: NfcTriagePayload, NfcGuardianPayload, nfc_guardian_alias.dart, NfcPayloadCodec, NfcPayloadService, the NDEF record, guardianPayloadBudget;
    • the screens that write chips (registration, profile actions) and those that show what is read;
    • the backend merge (record_merger.py, patient_service.py, schemas/patient.py), to know what a copy may leave out.

    Out of scope:

    • key management and the design of the encryption (ADR 003, nfc-encryption.md);
    • the UI beyond which fields it shows.

    The pipeline as built¶

    Paths and lines at 25ec698.

    1. Payload maps. Two builders produce them:
    2. NfcTriagePayload.buildPatientPayload writes the triage with hand-shortened keys (fn, dob, alg…).
    3. NfcGuardianPayload.buildGuardianPayload writes the whole PatientFullRecord.toJson(), minus the consent signature and the AI metadata of diagnoses, with at most the three most recent consultations and vaccines. aliasGuardianPayload then shortens 110 keys and adds a $v: 2 marker.
    4. Encoding (nfc_payload_codec.dart): CBOR (package cbor 6.5.1, which stores each float in its shortest lossless form), then raw DEFLATE level 9 (line 236, no dictionary), then AES-256-GCM. The encryption adds a 12-byte nonce and a 16-byte tag; from key version 1 on, also a 3-byte HW + version header, and the chip binding of ADR 003.
    5. Record (nfc_payload_service.dart:81, :92): one MIME record of type application/vnd.hwb.triage or application/vnd.hwb.guardian.
    6. Fit (nfc_guardian_payload.dart:117-129): the guardian copy is trimmed to the capacity the chip reports, alternating the oldest consultation and vaccine and stopping at the first combination that fits. The triage is never trimmed.
    7. Read (readHwbChip): find the MIME record, decrypt with the chip binding and then without it, inflate, decode the CBOR, rebuild the record.

    Method¶

    Corpus. 600 invented records: 400 for training dictionaries and models, 200 for measuring. corpus_test.dart generates them with the app's own builders.

    • Fields are filled as the registration, consultation and vaccine screens fill them.
    • Clinical text is Spanish, combined from phrase lists. Each list has entries that appear only in the test split, so dictionaries are not measured against their own training data.

    Validation.

    • The benchmark reproduces the app's CBOR byte for byte for all 3,000 payloads (triage and guardian copies with 0 to 3 consultations and vaccines).
    • Every DEFLATE figure comes from the app's own dart:io zlib: Python's zlib 1.2.12 gives other sizes for the same bytes (−10 to +29 B on a 1 KB payload).
    • Every compressed payload with a dictionary was decompressed back and compared.

    Encodings measured.

    Triage Guardian copy
    Current T0 G0 (G0b: the alias table completed)
    Without redundant keys: a key is dropped only if what the app reconstructs stays identical, checked record by record T1 G1
    + integer keys T2 G2
    + typed values (UUIDs and chip UIDs as bytes, dates as day counts, date-times as minutes and offset, digit strings as integers) T3 G3
    + catalog codes for closed or public lists T4 G4

    Compressors.

    • none;
    • DEFLATE-9 as today;
    • DEFLATE-9 with an 8 KB or 32 KB dictionary made of training payloads;
    • zstd 1.5.7 at level 19, with or without a trained dictionary, without its 4-byte magic number;
    • brotli 1.2.0 at quality 11, with or without the same 32 KB dictionary;
    • raw LZMA2;
    • a context-model reference: bytes context of orders 0–3, Witten-Bell interpolation, trained on the training split, ideal arithmetic-coding length. A reference for what a trained statistical coder reaches, not a theoretical bound.

    Capacity. The app's budgets, guardianPayloadBudget: 458 B on an NTAG215 and 838 B on an NTAG216 (capacity minus the MIME record overhead). Two algorithms: the app's own, and a full search over (consultations, vaccines) for what is achievable.

    Compressors, at equal encoding¶

    Compressed payload, median (90th percentile), current encoding:

    Triage 122 (167) 120 (154) 48 (61) 124 (155) 56 (68) 121 (156) 63 (76) 124 (160) 28 (36)
    Guardian 1 + 1 1,556 (1,769) 1,050 (1,175) 392 (453) 1,065 (1,192) 324 (378) 995 (1,111) 388 (447) 1,080 (1,201) 286 (351)

    • Without a dictionary every compressor lands in the same place. On the triage DEFLATE is even the smallest; on the guardian copy brotli saves 5 to 7 %.
    • With a dictionary, every compressor drops to between 30 % and 52 % of its size without one. zstd with a dictionary is 8 to 19 % smaller than DEFLATE with a dictionary on the guardian copy, and larger on the triage.
    • Typed values bring DEFLATE with a dictionary within reach of the context model. With G3 encoding: 531 B against 544 B for 3 + 3, and 310 B against 277 B for 1 + 1.

    Current guardian copy with 3 + 3, after DEFLATE, share of the total that removing each part saves:

    Part Share
    Consultations (medicalHistory) 61 %
    Free clinical text 32 %
    Vaccines 12 %
    UUIDs of visits and vaccines 10 %
    Patient info 9 %
    Guardian info 6 %
    Dates and date-times 4 %
    Key names 3 %

    Removing a part saves less than its own size when it shares text with the rest, so the shares do not add up to 100 %.

    Findings¶

    H1. No preset dictionary. - Where: nfc_payload_codec.dart:236, ZLibEncoder(level: 9, raw: true). - Effect: at these sizes DEFLATE has no history of its own. The triage goes from 122 B to 120 B; with an 8 KB dictionary, to 55 B, without changing anything else. This is the largest lever.

    H2. dart:io ignores a dictionary on raw streams. - What happens: ZLibEncoder(raw: true, dictionary: d) returns the same bytes as without it, and the decoder ignores it too, so a round-trip test passes while the dictionary does nothing. - What works: a raw stream primed by compressing the dictionary and flushing with Z_SYNC_FLUSH (the stream deflateSetDictionary produces, readable by any zlib, with no extra bytes), or the zlib format with the dictionary (4 B more). The benchmark checked both on 18,060 payloads.

    H3. The fit leaves out what fits. - Where: nfc_guardian_payload.dart:117-129. - What happens: the trim alternates consultations and vaccines and stops at the first combination that fits. A consultation is several times larger than a vaccine, so vaccines are dropped in step with consultations. - Effect: on an NTAG216 the copy keeps 1.03 vaccines on average where 2.35 fit.

    H4. Defaults are written. - What: keys whose absence leaves the reconstructed record identical are written to every chip. For example type: "Consultation", careModality: "01", diagnosisType: "01", status: "completed", intent: "order", country: "COL", policyVersion: "v1.1", and empty lists (riskFactors, prescriptions, allergies…). - Effect: 6 to 9 % after DEFLATE, almost nothing once there is a dictionary.

    H5. Values stored as text. - What: UUIDs of visits and vaccines, dates, date-times and digit strings are written as text. - Effect: UUIDs are 10 % of a compressed guardian copy and dates 4 %. Typed values save 16 to 24 % even with a dictionary. - Alias table: it misses 9 keys the copy carries (prescriptions, frequency, duration, route, intent, weight, height, isBloodTypeCertified, baseVersion); completing it saves 2 to 5 %.

    H6. A long record type. - Where: nfc_payload_service.dart:81 and :92. - Effect: the MIME types cost 29 to 34 B per chip; an 8-character external type costs 11 to 14 B. - Constraint: nothing in the OS depends on the type (no NFC intent filters, AndroidManifest.xml:36-45), but from key version 1 the type is part of the chip binding's associated data.

    H7. btC is written and never read. - Where: the triage stores btC: true for a blood type confirmed by a test (nfc_triage_payload.dart:40), but fromPayload (:90) ignores it. - Effect: a record rebuilt from the patient device alone always shows the blood type as reported.

    H8. Nothing measures real payloads. - What: the size of a write only shows when it does not fit (nfc_guided_write.dart:382). - Effect: nothing calibrates the decisions above, and the length of real clinical notes, which decides most of the remaining uncertainty, is unknown.

    Already right. - Serialize, then compress, then encrypt: encrypted bytes do not compress. - CBOR is about 20 % smaller than JSON. - Raw DEFLATE carries no checksum of its own, which is correct because AES-GCM authenticates. - Level 9. - The 28 B of AES-GCM are the standard parameters and should stay.

    Recommendations and where they went¶

    # Recommendation Pull request
    1 Fit: give back what still fits after the trim (H3) guane-hwb/health-without-borders-frontend#182
    2 Payload format 2: typed values, preset dictionary, external record (H1, H2, H5, H6); phase 1 reads both formats and writes format 1 guane-hwb/health-without-borders-frontend#185 (ADR 005)
    3 Measure real payloads: write-size telemetry and a tool for existing records (H8) guane-hwb/health-without-borders-frontend#186 and guane-hwb/health-without-borders#106
    4 Read btC (H7) guane-hwb/health-without-borders-frontend#183
    5 Decide what the guardian copy carries offline guane-hwb/health-without-borders-frontend#184 (ADR 006)

    Not recommended:

    • Moving to zstd or brotli: a native dependency for a single-digit gain over DEFLATE with a dictionary.
    • Shortening the AES-GCM nonce or tag: a security trade-off for 4 to 7 B.
    • Catalog codes for now: 4 to 6 % with a dictionary, at the cost of shipping and maintaining catalogs in the app.

    Limits¶

    • The records are synthetic. Relative results hold, because the structure of the payloads is the app's own. Absolute sizes, and capacity above all, depend on how long real clinical notes are. The marginal costs above let them be scaled.
    • Dictionary gains on free text are bounded, not known. The synthetic text comes from closed phrase lists that the dictionary learns. The optimistic figure counts that; the pessimistic one gives the text no gain at all. Real notes fall in between. The tool of recommendation 3 measures them without any record leaving the authorised environment.
    • Chip capacities are those Android reports (492 and 872 B). Nothing was written to a physical chip.
    • The context model is a reference, not a bound. It is also trained on synthetic text.
    • The breakdown of where the bytes go uses Python's zlib. Read its shares, not its byte counts.

    Reproducing it¶

    See tool/nfc_compression_audit/README.md. Every step is deterministic, and results.md in that directory is the report this audit is based on.
